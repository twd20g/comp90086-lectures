One correspondence gives one row — the nine knowns on the left, the nine unknowns of F on the right.
A second correspondence gives another row of us and vs, and another zero on the right-hand side. It knows nothing about the first: the only thing they share is f.
Eight of them stack into an 8×9 matrix. Nine unknowns, eight equations — one short, which is exactly right, because F is only defined up to scale.
So f is whatever that matrix sends to zero: its null space. Eight independent rows leave a one-dimensional null space, and that single direction is F — which is why it is the eight-point algorithm.
