Part one

Points and lines

One coordinate system in which projection, translation and lines are all vectors and matrices.

An image point is a ray, not a pair of numbers

__EQ_HOMOGUP__ Write a 1 at the end. The pair becomes a triple, and that triple is the point’s homogeneous coordinate.
__EQ_HOMOGDOWN__ To come back, divide by the last coordinate — which puts the point on the plane z = 1.
__EQ_HOMOGSCALE__ Scale the triple and the λ cancels on the way back, so every non-zero multiple reads back as the same point.
  • So the triple is not a point on a plane but the ray from the camera centre through it. Scale along the ray is not information, because every point on it images identically
  • World points get the same treatment: (X, Y, Z) becomes (X, Y, Z, 1), four numbers up to scale

A line is a vector too

Projection becomes a matrix multiply

__EQ_PINHOLE__ __EQ_PINHOLEREAD__
  • Perspective needs a division by Z, and division is not linear. Homogeneous coordinates defer the division to the moment the answer is read back
  • Everything in between is matrix multiplication, which composes, inverts and least-squares like any other linear algebra
Part two

Rigid motion

Six numbers that say where a camera is and which way it is pointing.

Rigid motion in homogeneous coordinates: SE(3)

  • Homogeneous coordinates enable us to put rotation and translation into a single matrix
__EQ_SE3__ __EQ_SE3INV__
  • These 4×4 matrices are SE(3), the rigid motions: rotation and translation, no scaling and no shear. Six degrees of freedom, three each
  • It is a group, and that is what makes it useful — composing two motions is a matrix product, and undoing one is an inverse that can be written down
  • So a chain of camera movements is a chain of matrix multiplications
Part three

The camera matrix

Five numbers that belong to the camera, and six that belong to the moment.

Intrinsics, extrinsics, eleven degrees of freedom

__EQ_CAMERA__
  • Intrinsics K — focal length in pixels, principal point, and whatever the sensor does. Five numbers, and they belong to the camera
  • Extrinsics [R | t] — where the camera is and which way it points. Six numbers, and they belong to the moment
  • Together, eleven degrees of freedom. Calibration is the business of finding the five that do not change
  • A camera with known K is calibrated: an image point can be turned back into a ray in the camera's own frame

Calibration from a plane, photographed many times

  • Photograph a known planar pattern in many poses. Each photograph has its own unknown pose but shares the same K
  • Each view gives a linear constraint on K; enough views pin it down, and lens distortion is fitted afterwards and iterated
  • Why a plane makes this easy is the next section
Part four

The homography

Where the eight numbers of two lectures ago came from.

Put the plane at Z = 0 and a column drops out

  • Nothing stops us choosing world coordinates, so put them on the plane: every point of interest has Z = 0
__EQ_PLANEZ__
  • The third column of the rotation is multiplied by zero, so it never contributes. What is left is 3×3
  • A plane in the world maps to the image by a single 3×3 matrix. That matrix is a homography
__EQ_HEQ__
  • Only the ratios matter: scale H and every image point is unchanged. Nine numbers, eight degrees of freedom — and each correspondence gives two equations, so four points fix it. That is the N = 4 of the RANSAC loop two lectures ago

Rectification is a homography too

  • Last lecture ended by rectifying a pair of unaligned cameras onto a common plane, and called the transform projective without saying why
  • A homography is exactly a change of image plane about the same camera centre. Rotating a camera, or re-imagining its sensor in a different orientation, is a 3×3 on the image
  • So rectification is two homographies — one per camera — chosen to make the two image planes parallel to the baseline
  • No depth is involved, which is why it can be done before any matching
Part five

Epipolar geometry

What one point in one image says about the other.

The centres, the point and its images are coplanar

Epipoles and epipolar lines

Rotating XO′ into O’s frame

A cross product is a matrix

  • Crossing with a fixed t is linear in the other vector, so it can be written as a matrix
__EQ_CROSSCHAIN__
  • That matrix is written [t]×, so t × v = [t]×v
__EQ_EFROMTRIPLE__
  • E = [t]×R is called the essential matrix. l = E x′ gives the epipolar line in camera O corresponding to x′, and l′ = E⊤x gives the line in O′ corresponding to x
  • Every epipolar line passes through its epipole, so the epipoles are the null spaces of E
__EQ_NULLE__

Sideways motion is last lecture's stereo pair

  • Move the camera sideways and the epipoles go to infinity. The epipolar lines become parallel and horizontal — they are image rows. That is the debt from last lecture, paid
  • Move it forwards and the epipole sits in the middle of the frame. Points stream away from it: the focus of expansion
Figures: Hartley & Zisserman (2004)

Without calibration: the fundamental matrix

__EQ_FUND__
  • If K is unknown, fold it in. F relates raw pixel coordinates in one image to epipolar lines in the other
  • Seven degrees of freedom: nine numbers, less one for scale, less one because det F = 0 — every epipolar line passes through the epipole, so F cannot have full rank
  • F is what correspondences alone determine. E is what they determine once the cameras are known

Eight correspondences, one linear system

  • Each correspondence gives one linear equation in the nine entries of F. Expand x′⊤Fx = 0 and collect terms
__EQ_EIGHTROW__
  • Stack eight such rows and the answer is the null space of the resulting 8×9 matrix — which is why it is called the eight-point algorithm
  • With more than eight, take the singular vector of smallest singular value: the least-squares answer

Normalisation, rank two, and RANSAC

  • Normalise the coordinates first — translate to the centroid and scale so distances average √2. Pixel coordinates in the hundreds make the system hopelessly ill-conditioned, and this one step is the difference between the two pictures above
  • Enforce det F = 0 afterwards, by zeroing the smallest singular value. A least-squares F will not have it, and the epipolar lines will not meet in a point
  • And wrap the whole thing in RANSAC — the loop from two lectures ago, with 8 points sampled instead of 4

A plane or a pure rotation gives a homography instead

  • If the scene is planar, the correspondences satisfy a homography, and any F built from that homography fits equally well. F is not determined
  • If the camera only rotates, there is no baseline, no epipolar plane and no depth to recover — and the two views are again related by a homography
  • Both degenerate cases give a homography instead. Which is the honest answer to why the book cover worked so well two lectures ago and would fail here
Two views constrain depth only when the camera moved and the world is not flat.

From F to rotation, translation and a triangulated point

  • Knowing K and K′, recover E from F. Decompose E into a rotation and a translation — the singular value decomposition gives four candidate solutions
  • Three of them put the reconstructed point behind one camera or the other. Testing that — cheirality — picks the right one
  • Now both cameras are known, so each match gives two rays, and the world point is where they meet. Triangulation is the least-squares nearest point to both
  • But t is only known up to scale. Double the baseline and double every depth, and every image is identical. Two views give the shape of the world and never its size
One known length anywhere in the scene fixes it — which is exactly what a calibrated stereo rig's baseline is for.

Refining Street View positions

  • Detect and match features, RANSAC for F, convert to E with the known camera matrices, decompose into R and t
  • The epipole is the other camera, so the geometry places each camera in the other's image — more precisely than the GPS did

Beyond two views

  • Structure from motion — chain the pairwise geometry over many images and refine everything at once by bundle adjustment
  • SLAM — do it as the images arrive, and use the map built so far to place the next frame
  • Both are this lecture repeated, with the scale ambiguity resolved once and then carried

Summary

  • Homogeneous coordinates make projection and rigid motion linear, and give points and lines the same algebra: on-ness is a dot product, joining and meeting are cross products
  • A camera is K[R|t]: five numbers that belong to the camera and six that belong to the moment
  • A plane collapses that to a 3×3 — the homography, and its eight degrees of freedom
  • Coplanarity of the two rays and the baseline gives E and F, which turn a point in one image into a line in the other
  • From which: the camera motion, and the scene — up to one unknown scale
